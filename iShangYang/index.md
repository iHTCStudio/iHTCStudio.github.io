**Read Lord Shang as history — sharp, dated, and kept at a distance.**

iShangYang (爱商鞅 / 愛商鞅) is an **offline historical workshop** for the *Book of Lord Shang*. **No account required.** Twenty-four extant chapters ship on your device with **four layers**: original, vernacular, learning English, and Duyvendak 1928 public-domain English. Open about **60** reform edicts. Walk the **361–338 BCE** chronicle and a fourteen-person graph. Keep **Daily Reform** on the Home Screen. **No ads. No tracking. Data Not Collected.**

This is **institutional history**. It is **not** a prescription for how to rule today.

**At a glance:** 24 extant chapters · 1,222 sentences · 60 edicts · 30 chronicle events · 14 figures · Daily Reform · Reform Study · `ishangyang://` · EN / 简 / 繁.

## Getting Started

### 1. First launch

1. Install from the [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6813769520) and open **iShangYang**.
2. The welcome guide leads with four-layer reading, edicts, and the chronicle. Replay: **Settings → Help**.

### 2. Read today’s reform

1. Open **Home**. **Daily Reform** shows today’s chapter.
2. Random explores only — it does **not** replace today.

### 3. Edicts, chronicle & figures

1. Open the edict hall: sense, present reading, and a self-check How to use — not a governing script.
2. Open the chronicle (`ishangyang://chronicle`) and expand a year for the vernacular note.
3. Open the figure graph (`ishangyang://figures`). Sensitive measures carry an in-app caution.

### 4. Recite & Reform Study

1. The Proclamation Desk recites with line highlighting and a four-layer playback matrix.
2. **Reform Study**: fade, fill-blank, quiz, edict match, and Moving the Pole.

### 5. Privacy & sync

1. Core use is **offline** after install.
2. Optional **iCloud** syncs favorites, lists, notes, history, and streaks through **your** iCloud.

## Why you’ll want to download

- **Four layers, one desk** — including a 1928 public-domain English that the other five apps do not carry.
- **Edicts and years, not slogans** — the chronicle puts a sentence back in 361–338 BCE.
- **Distance is the point** — historical study, not advocacy of harsh penalties.
- **Privacy by design** — no account, no ads, no analytics SDK.
